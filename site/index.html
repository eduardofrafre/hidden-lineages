What this is not

  • These are candidates, not new species. One gene can mislead; naming a species takes more.
  • The data are thin. Most species have fewer than five barcodes here.
  • Tested on known cases, it found them. Its false-alarm rate is not measured yet.
  • Insects are not reviewed yet. 34 names wait for the same reading.
Set aside

Set aside

Three names that look more like labelling problems than hidden species.

The three names
  • Astyanax laticeps: BOLD:ABU7559 / BOLD:AAB3435, 16.8%, Deuterodon langei. Southern barcodes of the complex do support splitting P. laticeps, but a 17% gap with a Deuterodon record in the same BIN fits misidentified vouchers better than a cryptic Astyanax.
  • Corydoras paleatus: 2 → BOLD:AAC7264 (Corydoras ehrhardti). Rosso et al. report over 3% between southern C. paleatus and the rest of the range, so these records may be a lineage rather than a misidentification.
  • Rhamdia quelen: 5 → BOLD:AAA6323 (Rhamdia voulezi), 4 → BOLD:ACF1690 (Rhamdia branneri). Ribolli et al. work on Rhamdia lineages with BINs on SC material; either the vouchers are R. voulezi and R. branneri, or the three names do not separate in COI.
